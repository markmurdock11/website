Serifos

a week to be deeply present

Each day began with a yoga class. Then breakfast. Then free time. Then a yoga class. Then dinner. And in between, you were on the island of Serifos.

Anton and Tony, I think, share a name and a home and a relationship. Or at least the roots of a name and definitely the roots of a deep love. Anton and Chloe, I think, share a love, a love for teaching and a love for movement and a love for life. Anton is a hustler by nature, magnetic. A TV character embodied in reality, somehow better than anything fiction could come up with. White smile, eyes like an eagle, passionate, but sharp edged and confident while also being welcoming. An interesting combination. When he teaches yoga, it reminds me of reading a hard book -- I must pay attention, I must double back to concepts I didn’t quite get, I am stretched and I am working and I know if I do this thing, I will be better for it. Tony is compact and subtle, he moves in a tensile manner, like someone who spent years doing high flying acrobatics at the circus. His smile reaches his eyes and his voice cracks with sincerity. A yoga teacher by trade, he was there for Anton but also for us -- the icebreaker for the group, the glue for the day. Chloe is pregnant, near bursting, and when she teaches yoga it feels like a conversation over a warm cup of coffee on a Sunday afternoon. I want to stretch and sigh and I find myself thinking pleasant thoughts that gently push me towards a better life, a feeling of optimism. Her smile is BIG and it makes you feel like the only person in the room, despite it obviously being a class with 40 people. These were the men and woman that spent the better part of 3 hours every day sharing their practice of movement, of yoga, and also of presence.

One of my biggest takeaways from them is how similar they felt to the people I aspire to be like in totally unalike industries. They are just people who took something they are really good at and created a life around it. Sacred Fig is a business and Anton and Chloe through pure force of will and exquisite taste and mastery over craft so beautifully designed a week that I will never forget and made it seem easy. I always feel such awe in people who are such experts and this week really reminded me how much breadth there is in creating a life that is interesting and how much variance a human’s life can take in what they are going to get good at.

I spent a week at a yoga retreat with a group of people who were beautiful and powerful and strong and interesting but most importantly, they were, meaning they are. Meaning these people were present and full and so much fuller than any adjectives I could use to describe them. There was Sarah with an H who just quit her job to found her second business who is a mother of two and a partner of one and who glows with authority and was rosy with passion and a bit sweet in understatement and she was french and she was a bit nervous at first but then she blossomed and her idea is going to be a physical idea meaning something to do with luxury but premium luxury and it will be in Paris and maybe it will have something to do with wine or maybe it will be a retreat center and really it could be anything because Sarah, I have no doubt, will make any idea she finds worthy of her teeth sinking in a reality. And then there was Michele a restaurant owner and glowing with life who lives in Atlanta and is absolutely gorgeous and incredibly ripped and consistently didn’t answer questions about her restaurants and hemmed and hawed that they were small and tiny and that was such a giveaway that they were big and important because I too have southern roots and know how to flirt in humility (she was definitely flirting with me). Michele had a brain aneurism a few years ago and she made Emma cry when she told her story on the second to last night of the trip and I would’ve never guessed that such a strong woman had been through such a hard thing and it reminded me that everybody has so much depth to them and that even when you think you know something or understand something about a person, there is so much more happening than meets the eye.

I could go on and on and on about the people I met here. Mariah is working on an article about AI ethics. Patrick likes ice cream, like a lot. Ann and Brady are star-crossed besties. Kara and Rachel are not impressed with the men in New York. Sara is similarly unimpressed with men! Donna, well Donna is Donna! Jo is questing around Greece, finding herself. Ale has met a man and his name, if I remember, is Franco and he seems dense personality wise. Jameson was on his second retreat and I bet it will not be his last. Julia is from Alaska and Galena and her followed our trip with a trip to Istanbul! Melina is a mother and is best friends with Tori and Bri and I didn’t get to hang out enough with them, but they absolutely made me laugh, swimming from shore to boat while carrying just above the breaks of the waves, a bottle of tequila. I don’t know, these are such little details but it was so good to meet these beautiful people and to exist in a way that I can remember them!

Feel the light and follow the energy. At the crown of your skull and also in the bottom of your stomach. Now breathe and think about the wild oregano. That thrives on the hillside with just a bit of water and the sun and the stars. And continue breathing, slowly to the count of four. And picture behind you all the people who have helped you get here and feel before you the potential energy of all the people you are soon to meet and above and below you wrap yourself with memory and with love and with whatever else to feel the gratefulness for the past and excitement for the future. And now take a half breath, with your hands up, palms to the sky, cupped, and now exhale fast, over and over again for 45 seconds and think nothing. Focus on the breath and push it out and out and out and out to the beat of the song. Let your diaphram do the work and your gut move — breath, breath, breath, breath. And now bring your hands to your chest and again think of the wild oregano and slow down and fade away and slow down and fade away and calm your thoughts and think of the wild oregano, that thrives with just a bit of sun and some rain and of course the stars.

Life without a phone is a life of presence. Thundercloud in my pocket and it smells like rain, let me check the weather, oh my brain, my brain it strains. Delighted at dinner. Conversation bouncing, not a screen in sight. A screen on the table, a blight, a blight. Breeze on my face on a boat. It is a great day. Bring out my phone, suddenly that feeling in my throat. A moment in between. An empty cup of coffee under a tree and I notice a leaf. It is green but a deep green: emerald in the light, forest in the shadow. I feel the need to photograph for future me, so I grab my phone and my body reacts: fight or flee? Being without the need for a phone for such an extended period of time was breathtaking -- I felt free. It was also humiliating. The device owns me. When I left it at the apartment, it took 10 minutes to wipe the anxiety away. When I brought it with me, I felt it suck away away all the special moments I had just opened up, tricking me into just one more scroll, a quick glance at the notifications. Serifos was a place to live without screens, a place to be and notice.

Books often find me at the right time. I buy a lot of them and start a lot of them, but leave many strewn around the apartment (sorry Emma), unfinished, but with their ideas percolating. I bought Infinite Jest in London and I thought it was a London book, but it was not. I then went to Berlin and thought to myself, wow, well, Infinite Jest is a Berlin book for sure -- psychedelic and such. But no, I couldn't get past the first page. But on Serifos, I think, perhaps because of my addictions being so far removed -- of work being on the backburner and my phone being mostly off -- that I was able to deal with Infinite Jest. It is a book that doesn’t end. A book that loops. Where simple moral platitudes (honesty is good, drugs are bad, family is important, hard work pays off) are masked by complex narratives, of too many characters and acronyms and footnotes. It is a book that forces the reader to follow and to think and to guess and to double back and to reread. I dreamed a lot while I was reading Infinite Jest. Weird dreams that I don’t remember, though I do remember dreaming and I rarely dream.

I do not think it was a coincidence I had my first trip on Serifos; the highest and lowest I have ever felt, induced by simple breathing led by Anton (two breaths in, one breath out) on the hillside of the island of Serifos.

Infinite Jest is a book that rewrites your brain if you allow it because it is a book of fiction that hides facts of life. There was not a better time for me to deal with Infinite Jest. There is a lot for me there: parallels to Hal and his sports upbringing ring true as a kid who grew up with a dad who played quarterback at the University of Texas. The sad addiction to screens and media and jobs, I obviously, as someone whose job is often to attempt to exert control over algorithms while also being the slave to the moment, to the always on nature of capturing sentiment over the scrollers -- it was a book for me. It also even put me in the right mindset for breathwork -- that I was reading about this twisted tale that treated the form of reading as a drug unto itself I think really opened me up to the possibility of letting go.

And Emma and I, it was our first time together, alone, with neither of us having a job calling for our attention and we relished and we relaxed in each other's presence. We sat by the pool and we read our books and we sat on our patio and we laughed and we walked everywhere and we did yoga with our mats right next to each other. And I fell in love with her even more than I had imagined I could.

One night, after a dinner upon a hill, overlooking the ocean, we walked home. It was a 45 minute walk, and it was dark, so dark, and there were no streetlights and very few cars and it was just us and it was after midnight and we walked. And the stars they shown so bright and we talked and we walked and I do not remember what we spoke of but I remember that she was wearing my favorite dress of hers and she looked so beautiful and she made me smile and it was a night, under those bright, bright stars on the island of Serifos, that I wished I could’ve frozen forever. I loved Serifos because it deepened my love for Emma. And it made me want to live a life more present and alive. It was a good trip, a trip to feel deeply present in a world that wants to distract you.
