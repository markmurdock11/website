## The atom

Every Kram.bio image is a diagram of an idea disguised as a landscape.

Build with five roles:

1. **Field** — a tactile lilac atmosphere.
2. **Ground** — a near-black place or recognizable human presence.
3. **Path** — orange movement, transmission, or connection.
4. **Witness** — one cobalt subject or discovery.
5. **Destination** — one acid-lime arrival, invitation, or current state.

This is not a “space” brand. The landscape gives an abstract idea lived context. The signal reveals the relationship inside it.

## Non-negotiables

- Use Spectral only, with Georgia and serif fallbacks.
- Use `#E5D5F1` and `#151419` for almost the entire composition.
- Use orange sparingly for energy. Use blue once. Use lime once.
- Keep at least 55% of the composition calm.
- Ground must touch an edge and occupy 18–42% of the frame.
- Every scene needs a terrestrial or human referent: terrain, coast, map contour, architecture, path, or portrait.
- Choose exactly one energy event: horizon, beam, portal, convergence, orbit, or transmission.
- Use no more than three path lines.
- Paths begin off-canvas or resolve at an event. They never float decoratively.
- Preserve asymmetry. Place the focal event 6–15% away from a conventional center.
- Add typography after creating the base image.
- Recompose for each ratio. Never merely shrink a desktop crop.

## Color

| Token | Hex | Job | Typical share |
|---|---|---|---|
| sky | `#E5D5F1` | atmosphere | 55–70% |
| paper | `#F7F1F8` | reading surface | alternate field |
| ink | `#151419` | ground and hard contrast | 25–40% |
| orange | `#FF681F` | movement and transmission | 2–8% |
| blue | `#175FF1` | subject or discovery | one mark |
| lime | `#D7FF2F` | destination or active state | one mark |

Tints may be made with opacity over sky, paper, or ink. Do not introduce new hues casually.

## Type

- Family: `Spectral, Georgia, serif`
- Weights: 400, 500, 600
- Display: line-height `0.62–0.95`; tracking `-0.04em` to `-0.09em`
- Body: line-height `1.35–1.55`
- Navigation: lowercase, 11–13px, weight 500
- Taxonomic labels: uppercase, 8–11px, tracking `0.08–0.14em`
- Social-card copy: eight words maximum

Hierarchy comes from scale, space, position, and contrast. Do not add monospace or a second display family to imply “system.”

## Construction pipeline

Create deliverables in three controlled layers:

1. **Raster base** — atmosphere, terrain, tactile texture, or portrait. No text, dots, or orbital lines.
2. **Signal overlay** — controlled paths, orbits, beams, and orange/blue/lime points.
3. **Typography** — add Spectral in HTML, Figma, Canva, SVG, or layout software.

Do not ask an image generator to create the complete finished graphic in one pass. That causes color, geometry, and typography to drift.

## Base-image prompt

> Create a sparse editorial landscape illustration on a soft lilac field with irregular colored-pencil and paper texture. Include near-black terrestrial ground with a strong silhouette and clear scale. Preserve a large calm atmospheric region. Add no text, logos, orbital lines, dots, interface elements, stars, rockets, or generic space imagery. Keep it tactile, high-contrast, playful, intelligent, and slightly strange.

After generation, add the path, witness, destination, and typography as controlled layers.

## LinkedIn profile picture

Job: recognition.

- Use Mark Murdock's real portrait. Never replace his primary LinkedIn portrait with an abstract planet or logo.
- Deliver at 1600 × 1600 PNG or JPG and verify inside a circular mask.
- Face fills 60–70% of the frame. Eye line sits around 42% from the top.
- Preserve real skin and the warm orange-red photographic background.
- Lilac may cool shadows or the outer field but must not wash the face.
- No path crosses the eyes or mouth.
- At most one thin off-axis orbit and one lime point, kept in the outer 12%.
- No text.

## LinkedIn banner

Job: place Mark inside the visual world without repeating the profile picture.

- Working canvas: 3168 × 792; export at LinkedIn's recommended 1584 × 396.
- Reserve the left 30% as calm atmosphere for the overlapping profile image.
- Place terrain and the focal event in the right 60%.
- Ground occupies 20–32% of the height.
- No baked text by default; the profile already identifies Mark.
- The banner and profile picture should feel like one scene without drawing a path into Mark's face.

## Editorial and social covers

- `1:1 post`: put the title in the calm field; keep the event in the opposite third.
- `4:5 post`: reserve the upper or lower 28% for optional type.
- `3:2 essay`: prefer no baked title when the publishing platform supplies one.
- `16:9 cover`: use one dominant event and no more than eight words.
- `9:16 story`: use a vertical path; protect the top and bottom 12% from essential content.
- A series repeats the grammar, never the exact landscape.

## Presentations

- Title slide: one dominant scene; title sits in the calm field.
- Content slides: use paper or ink reading surfaces. Do not put a landscape on every slide.
- Orange paths may connect arguments, chronology, or evidence.
- Lime appears once for the destination or conclusion.
- Data stays functional. Mountains are never chart decoration.

## Subject translation

- personal writing → a path through a real place
- archive → selected signals in a field
- work → a relay or launch across grounded infrastructure
- recommendation → a beam sent between distant bodies
- travel → local land contours, paths, flags, and motifs
- biography → portrait plus a lived landscape

## Visual modes

- **land** — travel, first attempts, lived context
- **human** — friendship, romance, community, biography
- **ideas** — reading, curation, archives, language
- **work** — launches, infrastructure, systems, evidence
- **signal** — publishing, recommendations, invitations
- **portrait** — Mark Murdock as the recognizable subject

## Copy modes

- **identity** — name and recognition; usually `MARK MURDOCK` / `KRAM`
- **thesis** — one sharp claim, eight words maximum
- **belief** — a personal conviction in Mark's existing language
- **archive** — a category, title, or saved object
- **future** — an ambitious direction or research frontier
- **invite** — one direct action
- **none** — image only; preferred when a platform supplies context

Do not invent filler to complete a composition. Pull from Mark's writing, beliefs, article titles, and existing site language first.

## Boundaries

Use:

- grounded terrain
- one conceptual signal
- tactile irregularity
- calm atmosphere
- strong silhouettes
- real portraits

Avoid:

- starfields, rockets, galaxies, zodiac forms, or atom symbols by default
- glossy chrome, glass, neon grids, or sci-fi interface panels
- decorative constellations with no semantic role
- equal color swatches in the finished work
- card grids, hard containers, or dashboard layouts
- generic startup copy or filler prose
- paths that float without an origin or destination
- altering Mark's recognizable likeness
